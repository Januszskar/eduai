Key Definitions & AI Literacy

Nine terms the guidelines rely on throughout. Flip each card, then check your recall before moving on.

Click or tap a card to flip it

AI Literacy
The skills to effectively use, interpret and evaluate AI tools — including knowing their limits (hallucination, bias) and integrating outputs critically.
Generative AI
Models that produce new text, code, images, audio or slides from prompts — e.g. ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, NotebookLM.
AI Disclosure
The formal UWC AI & Plagiarism Declaration that ensures students disclose GenAI use and affirm the originality of their work.
Human-in-the-Loop
A model where human experts remain actively involved in overseeing, validating and correcting AI-driven outputs before adoption.
AI Bias & Fairness
Recognition that AI can reflect or amplify training-data biases — requiring regular auditing and mitigation.
Responsible Use of AI
Ethical, transparent AI use aligned with learning outcomes — acknowledging contributions and limitations, and avoiding harm.
Data Privacy & Protection
Handling personal/sensitive data per POPIA (South Africa), GDPR where relevant, and UWC policy — via approved procedures only.
Prompt Engineering
Crafting and refining input prompts — using clear language, few-shot examples or templates — to get accurate, relevant output.
Academic Integrity (with AI)
Not misrepresenting AI-generated work as one's own; citing AI use appropriately; avoiding deceptive practices.

Why a shared vocabulary matters

Students and staff use these terms loosely in conversation, but the guidelines use them precisely — especially AI Disclosure (a formal, checkable act) versus casual mention, and Human-in-the-Loop (a governance requirement, not just good practice). Getting the definitions right is what makes the rest of this course make sense.
