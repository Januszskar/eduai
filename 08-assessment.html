Assessment & Feedback

How to keep assessment authentic, and where AI fits — and doesn't — in grading and feedback.

Assessment design strategies (Table 3)

Project- or problem-based scenarios tied to local/current contexts; emphasise analysis, evaluation and creative synthesis over recall; require drafts, peer review and revisions; link theory to personal experience or research settings.

Formative practice (take-home): ungraded exploration with safe AI experimentation. Summative mastery (invigilated): graded, supervised, AI-prohibited assessments that demonstrate unaided proficiency.

Grade part of the mark on process, not just outcome — prompt logs, drafts, reflection notes that show whether AI was used meaningfully and ethically.

Vivas, presentations and in-class quizzes verify understanding and authorship. Even brief interviews can reveal the depth of a student's knowledge.

Rotate datasets, case details or parameters each year or section; use random spot-checks with brief oral verification to deter generic AI responses.

Grading with AI assistance

Human-directed marking always takes precedence. AI may only refine or support human grading — you must review AI-generated feedback for fairness and clarity, disclose substantive AI use, and use rubrics that credit both AI transparency and human contribution. Example line: “Feedback provided by the lecturer, assisted by AI and reviewed by the lecturer.”

Scheduling matters too

Poorly staggered assessments across a programme put students under pressure that pushes them toward uncritical AI use. EDULT requests a schedule from each programme to keep assessments evenly spaced — if your module's deadlines cluster with others, flag it.
