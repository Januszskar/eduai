Foundations & Graduate Attributes

Before any rule about tools or citation, the guidelines start with intent: why the Faculty is doing this, and what kind of graduate it is protecting.

Faculty rationale

The Faculty's intent is to cultivate a scholarly environment where AI enhances research, learning and teaching, promotes equity, and supports quality — in a way that is developmental and supportive rather than punitive. The guidelines are deliberately non-prescriptive: they aim to expand pedagogical possibility without restricting academic freedom or compromising integrity.

The Faculty acknowledges that AI use has an impact on our planet — including water and power supply systems globally. Awareness of our own choices and AI footprint is part of responsible use.

What AI can't replace

The Faculty deliberately protects these activities

Greater weight is placed on activities AI cannot replicate. These aren't nice-to-haves — they are the Faculty's main structural defence against inauthentic AI use.

Practicum & fieldwork

Teaching practicums, reflective portfolios, classroom-based presentations, experiments and fieldwork.

Oral & live work

Oral defences and live presentations that require in-the-moment understanding.

Critical comparison

Critical analysis and comparison of the affordances of AI-generated text or artefacts.

Reflective portfolios

Reflective journals and evidence-based portfolios built over time.

Design & hackathons

Design-thinking challenges and interdisciplinary hackathons.

Graduate attributes

AI use must serve these UWC graduate attributes

Technological agency
Graduates should adapt and thrive within a complex, evolving technological society — harnessing innovation for the common good, with a strong ethical stance toward 4IR/5IR technologies.
Scholarship (a critical attitude to knowledge)
The ability to actively engage in generating and sharing knowledge through inquiry, critique, synthesis and research — applying both disciplinary and trans-disciplinary knowledge.
Inquiry-focused and knowledgeable
Graduates should be producers of knowledge, not just consumers of AI output — adept at research and inquiry within and across disciplines.
Ethically, environmentally and socially aware
Critical, responsible members of their communities, able to respond to unethical conduct, with human rights literacy and critical citizenship.
