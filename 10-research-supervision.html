Research Supervision

What you need to check with postgraduate students — from disclosure forms to publication norms — across the research lifecycle.

Supervisor checkpoints across the research lifecycle

All Master's and PhD dissertations must be accompanied by a multilingual AI disclosure form and a Turnitin similarity & AI-detection report — and the student should have access to that report throughout the process.

If AI processes human-subjects data (interview transcripts, intervention content), ethics approval must explicitly permit this. When in doubt, consult the Ethics Committee before proceeding.

Clarify ownership when AI generates images, code or other materials. Students must credit tools appropriately; consult the Research Office on uncertain IP questions.

AI may help summarise literature or suggest relevant articles, but students must read and synthesise original sources themselves, verify AI-generated summaries, and keep a physical or PDF copy of every reference they cite.

AI tools may aid data interpretation, but students must be able to explain and justify every analysis and script. AI usage must be reproducible and documented — e.g. “Tool X was used to cluster the dataset using these parameters.”

Journals generally prohibit listing AI as co-author and require disclosure of AI support in writing. Make sure students track usage and prepare disclosures accordingly, and that editors/examiners are informed of responsible-use expectations.

Assessment expectation

EDULT and EDUHD require oral defence for all Master's and PhD dissertations — and encourage the same practice at Honours level, to build the habit of defending one's own work early.

MOU reminder

All postgraduate supervision MOUs must include the AI Guidelines and required disclosure, discussed with the student up front, plus agreement to attend AI-related research training from CIECT and the Faculty Librarian.
