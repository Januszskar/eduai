Module 09 of 12

Data Handling & Tool Selection

The practical checks that keep you POPIA-compliant before a new AI tool ever touches student or research data.

Before you use any new AI tool

Check the UWC ICS/CIECT approved AI tools list first. If it's not on the list, don't upload institutional or student data to it — whatever its convenience.

Six checks before you commit to a tool

Cloud vs on-premise
Prefer UWC-hosted or offline tools for sensitive activities — they give greater data control and POPIA compliance.
Data minimisation
Input only essential data. Avoid entering student names or grades into external systems unless anonymised; use fictitious examples where possible.
Departmental oversight
Departments should track which AI tools are used in which courses — brief reports at Departmental meetings avoid duplication or conflict.
Chat history & training opt-out
Use trusted vendors that let you disable chat history or model training on your inputs.
Ethical risk review
For any new tool, assess bias, reliability and data exposure before adopting it Faculty-wide.
Vendor agreements & privacy terms
Read privacy policies carefully. Avoid tools that claim ownership of user data, lack deletion options, or are vague about data sharing.

Worth knowing

NotebookLM and Napkin AI are noted in the guidelines as tools where intellectual property stays with the producer — a useful default when IP protection matters for your data.
